ACME Healthcare – Identity Management and Access Control Policy

Project Overview

This project focuses on developing an Identity Management and Access Control Policy for ACME Healthcare.

The project addresses an important security challenge identified during an independent security assessment: former employees may retain active accounts and access to organisational systems after leaving the organisation.

This creates potential risks of unauthorised access, data exposure, insider threats and compromise of sensitive healthcare information.

The objective was to establish a structured policy and procedures for managing user identities, access privileges, authentication and employee account lifecycle management.

Project Objectives

* Establish secure identity management processes.
* Implement the principle of least privilege.
* Strengthen authentication through Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).
* Define employee onboarding and offboarding procedures.
* Reduce risks associated with inactive and orphaned accounts.
* Establish periodic access reviews.
* Improve accountability through monitoring and logging.

Security Areas Addressed

1. Identity Lifecycle Management

Defined procedures for creating, modifying, reviewing and disabling user accounts.

2. Access Control

Established access requirements based on job responsibilities and business needs.

3. Least Privilege

Ensured users receive only the permissions necessary to perform their assigned duties.

4. Multi-Factor Authentication

Established MFA requirements for privileged accounts, remote access and sensitive systems.

5. Employee Offboarding

Developed procedures for promptly disabling accounts and revoking credentials when employees leave.

6. Access Reviews

Introduced quarterly reviews to identify inactive accounts, excessive permissions and orphaned accounts.

7. Monitoring and Accountability

Established requirements for authentication logging, suspicious activity monitoring and incident escalation.

How I Approached the Project

I approached this project by:

1. Reviewing the ACME Healthcare security scenario.
2. Identifying identity management and access control weaknesses.
3. Defining appropriate security requirements.
4. Developing policy controls and account lifecycle procedures.
5. Assigning responsibilities to HR, IT, management and system owners.
6. Establishing monitoring, compliance and review requirements.
7. Producing a structured Identity Management and Access Control Policy.

Key Learning Outcomes

This project strengthened my understanding of how cybersecurity governance and identity management work together.

I learned that access management is not just about granting permissions. It also involves ensuring that access is reviewed, modified and revoked when necessary.

A former employee retaining access to organisational systems can become a serious security risk.
